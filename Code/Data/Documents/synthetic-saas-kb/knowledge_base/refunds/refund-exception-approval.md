# Refund Exception Approval

## Purpose

This policy governs refund requests that fall outside the Standard 30-Day Refund Policy, and goodwill grants issued as credit. Exceptions require appropriate approval before any refund or credit is issued. Approval of one exception does not entitle the customer to a repeat exception.

## When an Exception Applies

An exception is any refund or credit that the standard policy does not cover, including:

- Refund requests received after the 30-day refund window has closed.
- Prorated refunds not explicitly permitted by another applicable policy.
- Partial refunds outside the Partial Refund Scenarios policy.
- Goodwill credits granted for service or billing issues.

## Approval Levels

- Customer Support agents may not issue a refund or credit outside the standard policy on their own.
- Exceptions must be approved by the appropriate approver, typically a Billing Operations manager.
- Higher-value exceptions and any exception involving an Enterprise account are escalated per Billing Operations procedures.
- Approved exceptions are recorded in the customer's billing record with the approval and the reason.

## What an Agent Must Retrieve

Before an exception can be assessed, an agent must retrieve from the customer's billing record:

- Invoice ID and the charge date and amount involved
- The plan and seat count on the charge
- The billing cycle (monthly or annual)
- The original payment method
- The reason for the request and any supporting context

Approval decisions depend on the specific facts of each case; there is no blanket rule that all late or partial requests are approved.

## Goodwill Grants

- A goodwill grant approved as credit is held as a workspace credit balance.
- Credit balances are non-transferable, have no cash-out value, and are not exchangeable for a refund. See the Credit Balance Application policy.
- Goodwill amounts are determined case by case and are not a published schedule.

## Enterprise Contracts

- Enterprise contracts may contain their own refund terms.
- The customer's signed Enterprise agreement takes precedence over the standard self-service policy where the two differ, including over this exception process when the agreement defines its own path.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Approved exception outside the 30-day window

Long Tail Labs was charged $192.00 on invoice AF-2026-003960 for 8 Pro seats, with the charge posting on Jan 15, 2026. The customer requested a refund on Feb 25, 2026 — 41 days after the charge — outside the standard 30-day window. After escalation, a Billing Operations manager approved a one-time refund of the $192.00 charge as an exception. This is an example of an approved exception; it does not set a precedent for future late requests.

### Example 2 — Goodwill credit after an escalation

Cobalt & Co. reported a billing error through the Invoice Disputes process. Following review, a Billing Operations manager approved a goodwill credit of $96.00 — a value equal to 4 Pro seats at the monthly rate (4 × $24). The credit was held as a workspace credit balance and applied to the next invoice in order of generation.

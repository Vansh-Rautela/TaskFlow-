# Seat Limits & Add-Ons

A **seat** is one active user in an AcmeFlow Workspace. Seats are what paid plans are priced on, and seat changes during a billing cycle have specific billing consequences. This document explains how many seats each plan includes, how additional seats are priced, how proration works, and how removing seats affects future invoices.

## Included Seats by Plan

- **Free**: up to **3 users** included at $0 per month. There is no way to add seats on the Free plan; a 4th user requires an upgrade to Pro.
- **Pro**: billed per user. Every active user is a seat on the Pro invoice, priced at **$24 per user per month** (monthly billing) or **$20 per user per month** effective rate (annual billing, billed annually in advance).
- **Enterprise**: seat counts are defined by the **contract-specific seat commitment** in the signed agreement. There is no universal Enterprise seat count.

The number of users in the workspace determines the seat count on the next invoice. Billing staff should confirm the current active user count rather than assume a fixed number.

## Additional Seats

- On Pro, each seat beyond the current count is billed at the standard per-user rate of the workspace's billing frequency. There are no discounted seat blocks on the self-service tier.
- Enterprise agreements may include volume discounts for larger seat counts; these are negotiated, never a published rate.

Adding seats during an active billing cycle may generate a **prorated charge** for the portion of the cycle remaining. The full per-seat rate applies from the next renewal.

## Proration When Adding Seats

When seats are added mid-cycle on a Pro plan:

- The new seats are active immediately.
- A prorated charge covers the new seats from the add date to the end of the current billing cycle.
- From the next renewal, the new full seat count is billed for the entire cycle.

The prorated charge appears as a separate line item on the next invoice. See "Invoice Generation & Delivery" for how proration appears on invoice line items.

## Removing Seats

- Removing seats **normally affects future billing only**.
- The current cycle is not retroactively adjusted, and unused time on removed seats is not automatically refunded unless the applicable policy explicitly permits it.
- The reduced seat count is reflected starting at the next renewal.

## Minimum Seats

- **Free** and **Pro** (self-service) have no minimum seat requirement; a Pro workspace can bill for a single user.
- **Enterprise** agreements may define a minimum seat commitment. When an Enterprise contract includes one, the customer is billed for at least that many seats regardless of actual active users. Any minimum is per the signed agreement.

## Enterprise Seat Commitments

- Seat commitments, minimums, and volume discounts are set in the signed Enterprise agreement.
- The customer's signed Enterprise agreement takes precedence over the standard self-service policy where the two differ.
- When an agent discusses Enterprise seats, they must reference the customer's agreement terms rather than standard policy.

## Examples

**Example — adding seats with proration.** Greenfield Media is on the monthly Pro plan with 8 seats at $24 per seat, renewing on the 1st of each month. On February 10 they add 2 seats, bringing the total to 10. The prorated charge covers 19 of the 28 days remaining in the February cycle: 2 × $24 × (19 ÷ 28) = **$32.57**. The March 1 invoice includes this prorated line item, and the full 10-seat charge of 10 × $24 = **$240.00** applies from March.

**Example — removing seats.** Cedar Peak Consulting removes 2 seats from their 10-seat monthly Pro plan on the 15th of the month. Because removal affects future billing only, the current cycle still bills 10 seats; the next renewal bills 8 seats at 8 × $24 = **$192.00**. No refund is issued for the unused portion of the current cycle under the standard policy.

**Example — Enterprise seat commitment (labeled example only).** Under an example agreement, Blue Harbor Logistics commits to a minimum of 150 seats at a negotiated per-user rate. Even if active usage drops below 150 users, the agreement requires billing for the 150-seat commitment. This is an example of a negotiated commitment, not a standard policy.
